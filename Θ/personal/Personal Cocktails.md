## Smoke & Mirrors

> *A smoky, shadowed tribute to my favorite club in Chicago*

### Ingredients

| Ingredient  |                Amount |
| :---------- | --------------------: |
| Mezcal      |                  2 oz |
| Egg white   |                     1 |
| Agave syrup |                  1 oz |
| Cassis      |                3/4 oz |
| Lime        | 1/2 normal-sized lime |

### Method

1. Add all ingredients to a shaker and **dry shake**. *Careful—it gets messy.*
2. Add ice and shake again until well chilled.
3. Double strain into your glass.
4. Finish with bitters.

## Untitled

> _Warm and low-lit. Something sweet with a stone in the middle of it. It gets saltier the longer you sit with it._

### Ingredients

| Ingredient                    |      Amount |
| :---------------------------- | ----------: |
| Mezcal (espadín)              |      1.5 oz |
| Amontillado sherry            |      3/4 oz |
| Crème de noyaux               |      1/2 oz |
| Peach vinegar shrub*          |      1/4 oz |
| Sorghum syrup (cut 1:1, warm) | 1 bar spoon |
| Black walnut or peach bitters |    2 dashes |
| Salted-sakura ice cube**      |           1 |

*_Peach shrub: macerate ripe peach in sugar overnight, strain, cut with an equal part apple cider vinegar. Sharp, sweet, a little cruel._

**_Sakura cube: rinse two salt-pickled cherry blossoms, drop them in a 2" mold, fill slowly with cooled boiled water, freeze. Save a third blossom for the top._

### Method

1. Combine mezcal, amontillado, noyaux, shrub, sorghum, and bitters in a mixing glass.
2. Add ice and **stir 25–30 seconds**. No shaking. Nothing about this should be aerated or cheerful.
3. Strain over the sakura cube in a rocks glass.
4. Float the reserved blossom on the surface. It will slowly open.
5. Drink it slower than you want to. _That's the whole point._

### Why it's built this way


**The peach pit.** Crème de noyaux is made from the kernel _inside_ stone fruit — it's an old French liqueur distilled from apricot, peach, or cherry pits, and the almond flavor comes from a compound in the kernel itself. The kernels carry trace amygdalin, which the distillation renders harmless — the danger cooked out, the flavor kept. Sweet flesh, hard center, a little poison at the core made safe. There is no more literal way to put _a little pain_ in a glass. It's also naturally pink.

**The sorghum.** A Southern cane syrup, thinner and slightly sour compared to molasses, earthy rather than sweet-forward — it grounds the whole thing in Carolina without announcing itself.

**The blossoms.** Salt-pickled sakura develop coumarin during curing — an aroma somewhere between vanilla and faint bitterness, and the resulting brew is faintly salty as the petals unfurl. Your pink-flowered trees, preserved in salt. As the cube melts, the drink quietly gets saltier and more floral. **It is not the same drink at the end that it was at the start.** That's your confusion, and your heartbreak, engineered into the ice.

**The amontillado.** Oxidized, nutty, a little wistful — it makes the mezcal read as _warm_ rather than sharp.


### If you can't source things

- **Crème de noyaux** → amaretto, 3 parts to 1 part pomegranate juice.
- **Salt-pickled sakura** → a hibiscus-and-rose ice cube plus 2 drops saline solution.
- **Sorghum** → dark honey syrup. Not maple; too sweet.
