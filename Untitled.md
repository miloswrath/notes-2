# 03 — Normalized material timeline (Eastern Standard Time)

**Case:** BJones | **Author:** Codex, synthesis from completed Phase 2 reports | **Created:** 2026-09-13 (America/Chicago) | **Evidence copy:** `EC-BJONES-ZIP-ORIGINAL`, run `2026-09-12T194700-0500`.

## Bottom line

The written evidence supports a narrow, attributable sequence: Barry Jones created the Tomas account on January 27; subsequently, Tomas-owned artifacts show creation/editing of a disguised note in Barry's profile, two VeraCryptPortable executions recorded in Tomas's UserAssist hive, repeated execution and deletion of a batch file aimed at Barry's Documents folder, and creation of a different destructive batch file. `Malware.hidden` was created minutes after the VeraCrypt events, but its NTFS security context does not identify its individual creator. The evidence does **not** establish that `oops.txt.bat` ran, that its target XML files were deleted, that an inner VeraCrypt volume exists, or that Barry authored the incriminating note.

## VeraCrypt hidden-volume result

The outer volume decrypted without mounting and yielded only `TODO.txt` ("Send a message to Karl / download malware to the computer / delete all of the files.") plus high-entropy free space. A hidden-volume header was then tested non-destructively with 20 reasoned candidates, including `Lunes123`, weekday variants, names, blank, and two content-derived variants. Every attempt returned `No device header detected with this passphrase.` No inner header, master key, offset, or decrypted inner volume was obtained. This **does not prove that no hidden volume exists**; it proves only that none of those 20 passwords opened one. See `phase2/bjones-ioc-suspicious/shards/06-hiddenvolume-findings.md`.

## Important negative evidence

- No evidence names `oops.txt.bat` in UserAssist or Prefetch; the destructive command was found, not shown to execute.
- No Tor Browser installer or bundled-browser execution is corroborated by Prefetch/UserAssist, despite download and extraction evidence.
- No VeraCrypt driver/service, user Run/RunOnce persistence, or non-stock system Run-key persistence was found.
- The `Malware.hidden` filename is misleading: it is a confirmed VeraCrypt container, not an executable/malware sample.
